Sinking funds: the stress-free way to save

Most "surprise" expenses aren't surprises at all: holidays, car registration, annual subscriptions. A sinking fund turns them into small monthly amounts so they never hit hard again.

By the Rich With Reason teamNov 17, 20265 min read
The short version
  • A sinking fund is money you set aside each month for a known future expense.
  • Formula: cost ÷ months until you need it = monthly amount.
  • It keeps predictable costs from landing on a credit card or raiding your emergency fund.

Have you ever had a month where everything seemed to come due at once: the car insurance, a wedding gift, back-to-school shopping and an annual subscription renewal? None of those are emergencies. They're predictable. The problem is that they arrive in lumps, while your paycheck arrives in steady pieces. Sinking funds fix that mismatch.

What is a sinking fund?

The term comes from corporate finance, where companies set aside money over time to pay off a future debt. For personal finance, it simply means:

Saving a little each month for a specific, expected expense, so the full amount is ready when the bill arrives.

Sinking fund vs. emergency fund

Sinking fundEmergency fund
PurposeKnown, planned costsUnknown, unplanned costs
ExamplesHolidays, car registration, vacationJob loss, medical bills, a major repair
Spending itExpected, that's the pointOnly in true emergencies

Without sinking funds, predictable expenses often get paid from your emergency fund, which leaves you exposed when a real emergency hits.

How to calculate it

Take the expected cost and divide it by the number of months until you need it.

ExpenseCostMonths awayMonthly
Holiday gifts and travel$1,20012$100
Car insurance (paid every 6 months)$7206$120
Summer vacation$1,8009$200
Car maintenance and tires$90012$75
Annual subscriptions$24012$20

Total: $515 a month. That may sound like a lot, but those costs were coming anyway. Now they're spread out instead of arriving as a $1,800 shock in June.

Common sinking fund categories

  • Car: registration, maintenance, tires, insurance deductible, eventual replacement
  • Home: repairs, appliances, property taxes, HOA fees
  • Holidays and gifts: birthdays, weddings, baby showers
  • Travel
  • Medical: deductibles, dental work, glasses
  • Kids: school supplies, activities, camps
  • Pets: checkups and grooming
  • Annual bills: subscriptions, memberships, insurance premiums
  • Tech: replacing your phone or laptop
  • Big goals: a down payment or a new car

Where to keep sinking funds

Use a high-yield savings account so your money earns interest while it waits. To keep the categories separate, you have options:

  • Bucket features: Many online banks let you create named sub-accounts or "buckets" (Car, Holidays, Travel) inside one savings account.
  • Multiple accounts: Open a few free savings accounts and nickname each one.
  • One account plus a tracker: Keep everything in one account and track each category's balance in a simple spreadsheet.

How to start (even on a tight budget)

  1. Look back 12 months. Scan your statements for irregular expenses. Those are your sinking fund categories.
  2. Pick your top three. Don't try to fund 15 categories at once. Start with the ones that hurt most.
  3. Do the math and set up automatic transfers on payday.
  4. Spend from the fund when the bill comes, guilt-free. That's what it's for.
  5. Add categories as your budget allows.
Starting late? If the holidays are 2 months away and you need $600, you'd need $300 a month. If that's too much, save what you can now, scale back the spending a little this year, and start next year's fund in January at $50 a month.

Why it feels so good

Sinking funds change how spending feels. When you've saved $1,200 for the holidays, spending it is a planned choice, not a source of guilt. And when the car needs new tires, it's a mild annoyance instead of a crisis.

The bottom line

If you know it's coming, save for it. Sinking funds turn lumpy expenses into small, predictable payments, protect your emergency fund and keep your credit cards clear. Pick three categories and start this payday.
