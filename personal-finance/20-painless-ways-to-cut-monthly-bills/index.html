20 painless ways to cut monthly bills

You don't need to give up everything you enjoy to save money. These 20 moves trim recurring bills, most in under an hour, and the savings repeat every month.

By Jordan NoelNov 17, 20267 min read
The short version
  • Recurring bills are the best place to save. Cut once and you save every month.
  • Start with the biggest bills: insurance, phone, internet and subscriptions.
  • Send every dollar you save to a goal, or it will quietly get spent.

Cutting your latte saves a few dollars. Cutting a bill saves money every single month without any willpower. Here are 20 ways to lower recurring costs, grouped by category. Pick the five that fit you best and do them this week.

Subscriptions and memberships

  1. Audit every subscription. Look through three months of bank and card statements. Most people find at least one subscription they forgot about.
  2. Rotate streaming services. Keep one or two at a time. Binge a show, cancel and switch next month.
  3. Switch to annual plans for services you'll definitely keep. They're often 15% to 20% cheaper than paying monthly.
  4. Check for bundles and perks. Your phone plan, credit card or another subscription may already include a streaming service or cloud storage you're paying for separately.
  5. Use your library. Many libraries offer free e-books, audiobooks, movies and even online courses.

Phone and internet

  1. Try a prepaid or budget carrier. Many smaller carriers run on the same major networks for much less.
  2. Right-size your data. Check how much you actually use. Many people pay for unlimited data and use a fraction of it.
  3. Call your internet provider. Ask about current promotions or mention competitor prices. Retention departments often have discounts.
  4. Return rented equipment. Buying your own modem and router can pay for itself within a year.
  5. Check for low-income programs. Many internet providers offer discounted plans for qualifying households.

Insurance

  1. Shop your car insurance every year. Rates for the same coverage can vary a lot between companies.
  2. Raise your deductibles, if your emergency fund can cover them. Going from a $250 to a $1,000 deductible can noticeably lower premiums.
  3. Bundle home or renters and auto with one insurer for a multi-policy discount.
  4. Ask about discounts: safe driver, good student, low mileage, paperless billing, paying in full and safe-driving tracking apps.

Utilities and home

  1. Use a smart or programmable thermostat. Turning it back a few degrees while you sleep or are away adds up.
  2. Switch to LED bulbs and use smart power strips to stop "vampire" power drain.
  3. Check for utility programs. Many utilities offer free energy audits, rebates or budget billing to smooth out seasonal spikes.

Debt and banking

  1. Ask for a lower credit card APR. If you've paid on time, call and ask. It sometimes works, and it costs nothing to try.
  2. Look at refinancing high-interest loans or moving card balances to a 0% balance transfer offer.
  3. Stop paying bank fees. Switch to a no-fee checking account, opt out of overdraft coverage and set low-balance alerts.
How much could you save? Cancelling two subscriptions ($25), switching phone plans ($30) and shopping your car insurance ($40) adds up to about $95 a month, or more than $1,100 a year. Invested, that's real money over time.

A simple script for negotiating

Negotiating feels awkward, but it's often the fastest win. Try this:

"Hi, I've been a customer for [X] years. I'm reviewing my budget and I've seen lower prices from [competitor]. Are there any promotions or plans that could lower my bill? If not, I'd like to know my options for cancelling."

Be polite, be ready to actually switch, and ask to speak with the retention or loyalty department.

Make the savings stick

  1. Track your wins. Write down each bill you cut and how much you're saving.
  2. Redirect the money immediately. Increase an automatic transfer to savings or debt by the same amount.
  3. Set a yearly reminder. Prices creep up. Review your bills every year.

The bottom line

You don't have to give up what you love to save money. Cut the bills you don't notice, negotiate the ones you need and send every saved dollar to a goal. An hour of work this week can pay you back every month for years.

Common questions

What bills can I negotiate?

Internet, cable, phone plans, car and home insurance, credit card interest rates and some medical bills are often negotiable. Ask for current promotions and mention competitor prices.

What is the fastest way to cut monthly expenses?

Audit your subscriptions, shop your car insurance, and switch to a cheaper phone plan. Those three alone can save $100 a month for many households, and the savings repeat every month.

How do I find subscriptions I forgot about?

Scan three months of bank and credit card statements for recurring charges. Many banking apps also list recurring payments in one place.

Savings vary by provider and location. This article is for education only and isn't financial advice.
