How to start investing with $100

You don't need thousands of dollars to start investing. With $100 and the right first steps, you can own a slice of thousands of companies by this afternoon.

By the Rich With Reason teamNov 17, 20266 min read
The short version
  • Many brokerages have $0 minimums, no trading commissions and fractional shares, so $100 is enough to start.
  • Put it in a broad, low-cost index fund or ETF, not a single "hot" stock.
  • The habit matters more than the first amount. $100 a month for 30 years at 7% grows to about $113,000.

One of the biggest myths about investing is that you need a lot of money to begin. Not long ago, high minimums and trading fees made small investments impractical. Today, $100 can buy a diversified portfolio. Here's exactly how to do it.

Before you invest: a quick checklist

  • High-interest debt? If you're paying 20%+ on a credit card, paying it down is a guaranteed "return" that beats the stock market. Consider tackling it first.
  • Emergency cushion? Have at least a small emergency fund so you won't need to sell investments when something goes wrong.
  • Time horizon? Stock investing is for money you won't need for at least five years. Shorter-term money belongs in savings.

Step 1: Choose your account

AccountBest for
401(k) at workIf your employer offers a match, start here. The match is free money.
Roth IRALong-term retirement savings with tax-free growth. A great choice for many beginners.
Taxable brokerage accountFlexible investing for goals other than retirement. No contribution limits or withdrawal rules.

Step 2: Pick a brokerage

Look for:

  • $0 account minimums and $0 commissions on stock and ETF trades
  • Fractional shares, so you can buy $10 of a fund even if one share costs $500
  • Automatic recurring investments
  • A simple app and good customer support

Most large, well-known brokerages now offer all of these.

Step 3: Choose what to buy

With $100, keep it simple and diversified. Three beginner-friendly options:

  1. A total U.S. stock market or S&P 500 index fund or ETF. You own a small piece of hundreds or thousands of companies, for a very low fee.
  2. A total world stock index fund. U.S. and international companies in one fund.
  3. A target-date fund, named for the year you plan to retire, like 2060. It holds a mix of stocks and bonds and automatically becomes more conservative over time. It's a great "set it and forget it" option, especially in an IRA.
Skip for now: individual "hot" stocks, crypto, options and anything promising fast gains. With a small amount, one bad pick can wipe out most of it. Diversification protects you.

Step 4: Buy, then automate

  1. Transfer your $100 from your bank to your new account.
  2. Search for your chosen fund's ticker symbol.
  3. Enter the dollar amount to invest, using fractional shares if needed, and confirm.
  4. Set up a recurring investment, even $25 or $50 every payday.
  5. Turn on dividend reinvestment so dividends automatically buy more shares.

What $100 a month can become

Assuming an average 7% annual return (real returns vary year to year):

YearsYou put inCould grow to
10$12,000~$17,000
20$24,000~$49,000
30$36,000~$113,000

Most of that 30-year total comes from growth, not deposits. That's compounding, and it rewards starting early far more than starting big.

Beginner mistakes to avoid

  • Checking your balance every day. Markets swing. Daily checking leads to emotional decisions.
  • Selling in a downturn. Drops are normal. Historically, patient investors who stayed invested have recovered and grown.
  • Chasing what's hot. By the time something is trending on social media, the easy gains are often gone.
  • Paying high fees. A 1% yearly fee can quietly take a large share of your long-term growth.
  • Waiting for the "right time." Time in the market beats timing the market.

The bottom line

$100 is enough to become an investor today. Open the right account, buy a broad, low-cost fund, automate small contributions and leave it alone. The amount will grow with your income. The habit is what makes the difference.
