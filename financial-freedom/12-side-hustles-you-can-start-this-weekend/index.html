12 side hustles you can start this weekend

No big investment, no business degree. Here are 12 realistic ways to earn extra money starting this weekend, plus the tax basics most people miss.

By Jordan NoelNov 17, 20267 min read
The short version
  • The best side hustle uses skills or things you already have, so you can start fast and cheap.
  • Even $300 to $500 a month, if you invest it, can change your long-term finances.
  • Side income is taxable. Set aside 25% to 30% of what you earn for taxes.

A side hustle is one of the fastest ways to speed up any money goal, whether that's paying off debt, building an emergency fund or investing toward financial freedom. Investing an extra $300 a month for 10 years at 5% growth after inflation adds up to about $45,000. Here are 12 options you can realistically start in a weekend, grouped by what they need from you.

Use your time and energy

1. Pet sitting and dog walking

Apps like Rover and Wag connect you with local pet owners, or you can just tell your neighbors. Startup cost: basically nothing. Best for animal lovers with flexible hours.

2. Food and grocery delivery

Sign up with a delivery app and work whenever you want. Approval can take a few days, so apply now. Remember that gas and wear on your car come out of your earnings, so track your mileage.

3. Odd jobs and errands

Furniture assembly, moving help, yard work and mounting TVs are always in demand. Post on TaskRabbit or Nextdoor, or ask friends to spread the word.

4. House cleaning or car detailing

The supplies cost under $100, and repeat clients turn this into steady monthly income. Before-and-after photos make great marketing.

Use your skills

5. Tutoring

Help students with math, reading, test prep or a language you speak well. Sessions can be online, so your market isn't limited to your town.

6. Freelance writing, editing or design

Small businesses always need help with website copy, social posts, flyers and logos. Start with two or three samples, even ones you made for practice, and pitch local businesses or freelance marketplaces.

7. Virtual assistant work

Managing inboxes and calendars, entering data and booking travel can all be done remotely. If you're organized and good with common office software, this is a natural fit.

8. Teach what you know

Cooking, guitar, fitness, Excel, photography: if you're good at something, someone wants to learn it. Start with one small class or a one-on-one session.

Use your stuff

9. Sell things you no longer use

Clothes, electronics, furniture and baby gear sell well on Facebook Marketplace, eBay and Poshmark. It's a one-time boost, but it often funds your next hustle.

10. Flip thrift and yard-sale finds

Once you've learned what sells by clearing out your own closet, buy underpriced items and resell them. Focus on one niche you know, like sneakers, vintage clothes or tools.

11. Rent out what sits idle

Peer-to-peer platforms let you rent out your car, parking space, storage space or camera gear. Read the insurance terms carefully before you list anything.

12. Sell simple digital products

Printable planners, budget spreadsheets, templates or a short guide on something you know can sell on platforms like Etsy over and over. It takes up-front work, but there's no inventory.

Pick your hustle in 3 questions: What am I already good at? What do people already ask me for help with? How many hours a week do I realistically have? The best fit is usually where all three overlap.

The tax basics (don't skip this)

  • All side income is taxable, whether or not you get a tax form for it.
  • Self-employment tax applies. If you net $400 or more from self-employment in a year, you generally owe self-employment tax (about 15.3% for Social Security and Medicare) on top of income tax.
  • Know the forms. For 2026, payment apps and marketplaces generally only send a Form 1099-K if you receive more than $20,000 and more than 200 transactions. Businesses that pay you directly send a 1099-NEC once they pay you $2,000 or more. Not getting a form doesn't mean the income is tax-free.
  • Set money aside. Move 25% to 30% of every payment into a separate savings account for taxes.
  • Pay quarterly if you need to. If you'll owe $1,000 or more for the year, the IRS generally expects quarterly estimated payments.
  • Track your expenses. Supplies, a portion of your phone bill and business mileage may be deductible, which lowers what you owe.

Make the money actually matter

Side hustle income tends to disappear into everyday spending unless you give it a job. Open a separate account for it, set aside the tax portion, and then send the rest straight to your goal: extra debt payments, your emergency fund or an investment account.

Your weekend plan

  1. Saturday morning: Choose one hustle using the three questions above.
  2. Saturday afternoon: Create your profile or listing, or tell 10 people you're offering the service.
  3. Sunday: Open a separate savings account for side income and taxes.
  4. Next week: Do your first job, ask for a review, and decide what to do with your first $100.

The bottom line

The best side hustle is the one you'll actually start. Keep it simple, take care of the taxes and point every extra dollar at a goal. That's how a few weekend hours turn into real financial progress.

Common questions

Do I have to pay taxes on side hustle income?

Yes. All side income is taxable, even if you don't receive a tax form. If you net $400 or more from self-employment in a year, you generally also owe self-employment tax of about 15.3%.

How much should I set aside for taxes on a side hustle?

A common rule of thumb is 25% to 30% of what you earn. Keep it in a separate savings account so it's ready at tax time.

Will I get a 1099-K for my side hustle?

For 2026, payment apps and marketplaces generally send a Form 1099-K only if you receive more than $20,000 and more than 200 transactions. You still have to report income below that.

What is the easiest side hustle to start?

Ones that use skills or things you already have, like pet sitting, odd jobs, tutoring or selling items you no longer use. They cost little to start and can earn money the same week.
