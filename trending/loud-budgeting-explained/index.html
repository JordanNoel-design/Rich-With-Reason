'Loud budgeting' explained

It started as a TikTok joke and became a real money movement: saying your financial goals out loud instead of quietly overspending. Here's what loud budgeting is and how to try it.

By Jordan NoelNov 17, 20265 min read
The short version
  • Loud budgeting means being open about your money goals and saying "no" to spending that doesn't fit them, without embarrassment.
  • It was coined on TikTok in late 2023 by comedian Lukas Battle as "the opposite of quiet luxury."
  • The key idea: it's not "I can't afford it," it's "I'd rather spend my money on something else."

For years, social media pushed us to show off: designer bags, lavish trips and "quiet luxury" outfits that whisper wealth. Loud budgeting flips that script. Instead of quietly going along with expensive plans you can't really afford, you say out loud what you're saving for, and you make it something to be proud of.

Where it came from

At the end of 2023, writer and comedian Lukas Battle posted his list of what was "in" and "out" for 2024 on TikTok. "Loud budgeting" was in. He framed it as the opposite of quiet luxury. It started as a joke, but it struck a nerve with people tired of social pressure to spend, and the term spread quickly.

What loud budgeting sounds like

  • "I'm saving for a trip in the spring, so I'll skip the concert, but let's do a picnic instead."
  • "That restaurant's out of my budget this month. Want to cook at my place?"
  • "I'm trying to pay off my credit card by summer, so I'm doing a no-spend weekend."
  • "I'm putting that money toward my emergency fund instead."
The mindset shift: Loud budgeting isn't about scarcity. It's about priorities. "I don't have enough" becomes "I'm choosing something more important to me."

Why it works

  • It reduces social pressure. Much overspending happens because saying no feels awkward. Naming a goal makes it easier.
  • It creates accountability. Saying your goal out loud makes you more likely to stick with it.
  • It normalizes money talk. Friends often feel the same pressure and are relieved when someone speaks up.
  • It suggests alternatives. Saying no to a pricey dinner often leads to cheaper plans that are just as fun.

How to try loud budgeting

  1. Pick one or two clear goals, such as "save $1,500 for an emergency fund by March" or "pay off my $2,000 card balance this year."
  2. Make a simple budget so you know what you can spend on fun each month. The 50/30/20 approach is a good start.
  3. Practice your line. Have a friendly, ready phrase for when plans don't fit: "I'm loud budgeting this month!"
  4. Offer an alternative instead of just saying no.
  5. Share your wins. Celebrate milestones with friends, or online if you want to inspire others.
  6. Find your people. Friends or online communities with similar goals make it much easier.

Loud budgeting dos and don'ts

Do:

  • Speak for yourself and your own goals.
  • Stay flexible. A budget can include fun.
  • Suggest low-cost ways to stay connected.

Don't:

  • Lecture friends about their spending.
  • Overshare details you're not comfortable sharing, like your exact salary or debts.
  • Turn it into deprivation. If it makes you miserable, adjust the plan.

Related trends

  • No-spend challenges: going a day, week or month without non-essential purchases.
  • De-influencing: creators telling followers what not to buy.
  • Cash stuffing: using labeled envelopes of cash for spending categories.

The bottom line

Loud budgeting turns saving from something private and a little embarrassing into something confident and shared. You don't need to announce every decision. But next time a plan doesn't fit your budget, try saying so out loud. Your friends might be relieved, and your goals will thank you.

Common questions

What does loud budgeting mean?
