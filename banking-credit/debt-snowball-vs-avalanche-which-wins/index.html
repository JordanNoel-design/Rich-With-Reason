Debt snowball vs. avalanche: which wins?

One method saves the most money on paper. The other gets more people to the finish line. Here's how to choose, with real numbers.

By the Rich With Reason teamNov 17, 20266 min read
The short version
  • Snowball: Pay off the smallest balance first. You get quick wins that keep you motivated.
  • Avalanche: Pay off the highest interest rate first. It costs you the least in interest.
  • The money difference is often smaller than people expect. The best method is the one you'll keep doing.

When you have several debts, it's hard to know where to send extra money. Both of these methods start the same way. You pay the minimum on everything, then put every extra dollar toward one target debt. When that debt is gone, its payment "rolls over" to the next one. The only difference is the order of the targets.

The debt snowball

List your debts from smallest balance to largest, no matter the interest rate. Attack the smallest one first.

Why it works: You wipe out a whole debt fast, often within months. That means one less bill, one less due date and real proof that the plan is working. That momentum matters. Research on real borrowers suggests that fully clearing individual balances helps people stick with paying off debt.

The debt avalanche

List your debts from highest interest rate to lowest, no matter the balance. Attack the most expensive one first.

Why it works: The highest rate is the debt that grows fastest, so knocking it out first means you pay the least total interest. It's the mathematically best order.

Let's run the numbers

Here's one example with three debts and a total budget of $625 a month (the minimums plus $300 extra):

The avalanche saves about $55 here. The snowball gets you your first win 11 months sooner. That's the real trade-off. The gap grows when your debts have very different rates and big balances, and shrinks when the rates are close.

Example assumes fixed minimum payments and no new charges; figures are rounded.

How to choose

Pick the snowball if…

  • You've tried to pay off debt before and lost steam.
  • You have a few small balances you could clear quickly.
  • Juggling lots of bills stresses you out and you want fewer of them fast.

Pick the avalanche if…

  • You're motivated by numbers and can stay on track without quick wins.
  • One debt has a much higher rate than the rest, like a 29% card next to a 6% student loan.
  • Your balances are large, so the interest savings could be big.
Hybrid option: Knock out one or two tiny balances first for momentum, then switch to the avalanche for the rest. There's no rule against mixing them.

Make either method work better

  1. Stop adding new debt. Neither method works if the balances keep growing. Use cash or debit while you pay down.
  2. Keep a small emergency fund. Even $1,000 keeps a surprise car repair from landing back on a card.
  3. Automate the minimums. Then make the extra payment by hand each payday so you see your progress.
  4. Lower the rates where you can. A 0% balance transfer or a lower-rate consolidation loan can speed up either plan.
  5. Roll over every freed-up dollar. When a debt is paid off, its whole payment goes to the next target. Don't let it disappear into spending.
  6. Track it visually. A simple chart on the fridge or a spreadsheet you cross off works surprisingly well.

The bottom line

The avalanche wins on paper and the snowball often wins in real life. If you're sure you'll stick with it, choose the avalanche. If motivation has been the problem, choose the snowball and don't feel bad about the small extra cost. Getting to zero is what counts.

This article is for education only and isn't financial advice. If you're struggling to make minimum payments, a nonprofit credit counselor (look for NFCC members) can help you review your options.
