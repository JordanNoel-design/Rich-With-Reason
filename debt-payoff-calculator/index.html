How to use this calculator

Enter what you owe, the card or loan's APR (you'll find it on your statement) and what you plan to pay each month. The calculator works out how many months it takes to reach zero and how much of your money goes to interest along the way. If you have several debts, run each one separately or use the combined balance and an average rate for a rough picture.

A real example

Say you owe $6,000 on a credit card at 24% APR:

Going from $200 to $400 a month cuts more than two years off and saves about $2,000. Extra payments are powerful because every dollar above the interest goes straight to the balance.

Why minimum payments take so long

Each month, interest is charged first. On a $6,000 balance at 24%, that's about $120 a month before you've paid down a cent. If your payment is close to the interest charge, the balance barely moves. That's why a minimum payment can stretch a card balance over many years.

Four ways to get out of debt faster

  1. Pay more than the minimum. Even $25 to $50 extra a month makes a noticeable difference. Set it up on autopay.
  2. Choose a payoff order. With several debts, target either the highest rate first (avalanche) or the smallest balance first (snowball). See snowball vs. avalanche.
  3. Cut the interest rate. A 0% balance transfer card or a lower-rate loan can save hundreds or thousands. You can also call your card issuer and ask for a lower APR.
  4. Stop adding new charges. Pay with debit or cash while you're paying a card down, and keep a small emergency fund so surprises don't land back on the card.

Common questions

How long will it take to pay off $10,000 in credit card debt?

It depends on your rate and payment. At 24% APR, paying $400 a month takes about 3 years and roughly $4,000 in interest. Paying $500 a month cuts that to about 2 years 2 months and roughly $2,900 in interest.

How is credit card interest calculated?

Most cards divide your APR by 365 to get a daily rate and charge it on your average daily balance. As a rough monthly estimate, multiply your balance by your APR and divide by 12. At 24% APR, a $6,000 balance costs about $120 a month in interest.

Should I pay off debt or save money first?

Most people do best by keeping a small starter emergency fund of $1,000 to $2,000, getting any employer 401(k) match, and then attacking high-interest debt like credit cards before saving or investing more.

Does paying off debt raise your credit score?

Usually, yes. Paying down credit card balances lowers your credit utilization, one of the biggest factors in your score. Keep old no-fee cards open after paying them off so your available credit stays high.
