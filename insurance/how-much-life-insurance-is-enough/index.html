How much life insurance is enough?

Too little leaves your family exposed; too much wastes money every month. Here's a simple way to calculate the coverage you actually need.

By Jordan NoelNov 17, 20266 min read
The short version
  • If no one depends on your income, you may not need life insurance at all.
  • A quick rule of thumb is 10 to 12 times your income, but a needs-based calculation is more accurate.
  • Add up what your family would need, subtract what you already have, and round up.

Life insurance is about one question: If I died tomorrow, how much money would the people who depend on me need? Here's how to answer it.

First: do you need life insurance?

You probably do if:

  • You have children or other dependents.
  • A spouse or partner relies on your income to pay the mortgage or bills.
  • Someone co-signed a debt with you, like a private student loan.
  • You're a stay-at-home parent. Replacing childcare and household work costs real money.

You may not if: you're single with no dependents, or you already have enough savings to cover your family's needs.

Method 1: The quick rule of thumb

Multiply your yearly income by 10 to 12. If you earn $70,000, that's $700,000 to $840,000. It's a reasonable starting point but ignores your debts, savings and family situation.

Method 2: The DIME method

DIME stands for Debt, Income, Mortgage and Education. Add up:

CategoryWhat to includeExample
DebtCar loans, credit cards, personal loans, plus final expenses$30,000
IncomeYour income × the number of years your family would need it$70,000 × 10 = $700,000
MortgageRemaining mortgage balance$250,000
EducationExpected college or school costs for your kids$100,000
Total need$1,080,000

Step 3: Subtract what you already have

  • Savings and investments your family could use
  • Existing life insurance, including from work (but remember it usually ends if you change jobs)
  • Social Security survivor benefits, which can be significant for families with young children
Example continued: Total need of $1,080,000, minus $80,000 in savings and $70,000 of work coverage, leaves $930,000. Round up and this family might choose a $1 million term policy.

Adjust for your situation

  • Your partner's income: If your partner earns a good income, they may need less replacement income from your policy.
  • Childcare: If your death would mean paying for childcare that you currently provide, add it.
  • Inflation: A payout that seems large today buys less in 15 years. Rounding up helps.
  • Your plan for the money: If your family would invest the payout and draw from it, a larger amount can produce ongoing income.

Don't forget a stay-at-home parent

A parent who doesn't earn a paycheck still provides enormous value: childcare, transportation, cooking and household management. Replacing those services can cost tens of thousands of dollars a year. Many families insure both parents.

How long should coverage last?

For most families, term life insurance is the best fit. Choose a term that lasts until:

  • Your youngest child is financially independent.
  • Your mortgage is paid off.
  • Your savings would be enough to support your family.

Because term life is relatively inexpensive for healthy people, buying a little more coverage than you think you need is often affordable.

Review it regularly

Revisit your coverage after major life events:

  • Marriage or divorce
  • A new baby or adoption
  • Buying a home
  • A big raise or a career change
  • Paying off major debts

Common mistakes

  • Relying only on work coverage, which is often just 1 to 2 times your salary.
  • Insuring only the main earner.
  • Waiting. Premiums rise with age and health issues.
  • Not naming beneficiaries or forgetting to update them after life changes.
  • Naming a minor child as a direct beneficiary. Consider a trust or a custodian instead. An estate attorney can help.

The bottom line

Use 10 to 12 times your income as a starting point, then refine it with the DIME method and subtract what you already have. The right amount lets the people you love keep their home, stay on track and grieve without money worries.

Common questions

How much life insurance do I need?

A quick rule of thumb is 10 to 12 times your income. For a more accurate number, add up your debts, the income your family would need, your mortgage and education costs, then subtract savings and existing coverage.

Is life insurance through work enough?

Usually not. Employer coverage is often only 1 to 2 times your salary and typically ends when you leave the job.

Do stay-at-home parents need life insurance?

Often, yes. Replacing childcare, transportation and household work can cost tens of thousands of dollars a year, so many families insure both parents.
