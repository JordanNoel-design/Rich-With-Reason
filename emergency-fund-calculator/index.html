How much should you save?

The usual target is 3 to 6 months of essential expenses, not your full income. Where you land in that range depends on how steady your income is and who depends on you:

Your situationMonths to aim for
Stable job, two incomes, no kids3
Stable job, one income or kids4–6
Commission, freelance, gig or seasonal income6–12
Self-employed or business owner6–12

What counts as an essential expense?

Only the bills you'd still have to pay if your income stopped: rent or mortgage, utilities, phone and internet, groceries, transportation, insurance, minimum debt payments and childcare. Leave out dining out, travel and shopping, because those are the first things you'd cut in a real emergency.

Example: If your essentials add up to $3,200 a month and you want 6 months of cover, your target is $19,200. With $2,000 saved and $300 going in each month, you'd reach it in about 4 years 10 months. Bump that to $500 a month and it's under 3 years.

Build it in stages

  1. Starter fund: $1,000 to $2,000. Enough for most car repairs and surprise bills.
  2. One month of essentials. A short gap in pay won't sink you.
  3. Three months. You're covered for most job changes and medical surprises.
  4. Your full target. Then send that monthly savings toward investing and other goals.

Where to keep it

Keep your emergency fund somewhere safe and easy to reach, but not too easy. A high-yield savings account at an FDIC-insured bank, separate from your checking account, is the usual choice. Don't keep it in stocks: emergencies and market crashes often arrive together. For the full guide, read How much should be in your emergency fund?

Common questions

How much should I have in my emergency fund?

Aim for 3 to 6 months of essential expenses. Go toward 6 to 12 months if your income is irregular, you're self-employed or you're the only earner in your household. Start with a $1,000 to $2,000 starter fund.

Is $10,000 enough for an emergency fund?

It depends on your expenses. If your essentials cost about $3,300 a month, $10,000 covers roughly 3 months, which is a solid base for someone with a stable job. If your essentials are $5,000 a month, it covers about 2 months.

Where should I keep my emergency fund?

In a high-yield savings account or money market account at an FDIC- or NCUA-insured bank or credit union. It keeps your money safe, earns interest and lets you reach it within a day or two.

Should I build an emergency fund or pay off debt first?

Build a small starter fund of $1,000 to $2,000 first, so a surprise bill doesn't go on a credit card. Then focus on high-interest debt, and finish your full emergency fund after that.
